## An outbreak that changes your world

**Pathogenesis** is an **Ovomorphosis addon** inspired by *Prometheus* and *Alien: Covenant*, bringing pathogen contamination, environmental outbreaks, and the Neomorph life cycle into Minecraft.

Discover an abandoned dig site and be careful to not disturb that which should have been left buried. 

**Contain it early. Once the ecosystem takes hold, cleanup becomes a fight for the land itself.**

## At a glance

* **Exploration-driven outbreaks** — Discover pathogen containment sites and dangerous biological material.
* **Source containment** — Stabilize and safely recover sources before leakage becomes an established outbreak.
* **Environmental contamination** — Pathogen spreads through soil, vegetation, and water, changing the surrounding landscape.
* **Living pathogen ecology** — Growths and spore plants help an outbreak develop into a self-sustaining ecosystem.
* **Exposure and infection** — Contaminated environments threaten living hosts; heavy exposure accelerates existing infections.
* **Neomorph life cycle** — Infected Host → Bloodburster → Neophyte → Neomorph.
* **Contaminated wildlife and carcasses** — Sick animals, host deaths, and feeding sites leave evidence and create further contamination pressure.
* **Climate interaction** — Cold suppresses activity, contaminated water can freeze into dormant ice, and thawing brings the threat back.
* **Field investigation** — Use the Pathogen Sampler to assess contamination, source conditions, and outbreak activity.
* **Containment and cleanup** — Remove sources, destroy supporting flora, and sterilize contaminated terrain to drive outbreaks toward collapse.

## How an outbreak begins

### 1. Discover the source

The threat begins through exploration.

Containment sites can hold pathogen sources whose condition determines how urgently you need to act. A sealed discovery is an opportunity to investigate. A damaged or leaking source is a problem that can spread beyond the site.

Recovering dangerous material requires care. Pathogen ampules are fragile, and mishandling them can turn a discovery into an outbreak.

### 2. The environment becomes contaminated

A leaking or ruptured source can contaminate nearby terrain.

Soil changes, pathogen growth appears, and spore flora creates new exposure hazards. Contaminated water can carry the outbreak along nearby shorelines and into neighboring water sources.

The danger extends beyond the place where it started.

### 3. Living hosts become infected

Exposure builds through contact with contaminated environments and pathogen hazards.

Wildlife can show signs of contamination through sickness and altered behavior. Infected hosts progress through increasingly dangerous stages, eventually producing a **Bloodburster** if the infection is allowed to reach its terminal stage.

Bloodbursters grow into **Neophytes**, then mature into **Neomorphs**. Feeding and host deaths can leave contaminated remains.

### 4. The outbreak sustains itself

Early outbreaks depend on their source. Mature outbreaks can develop enough pathogen flora to keep spreading independently.

Removing the source is still important, but an established ecosystem may survive without it. Cleanup must also deal with the contaminated terrain and flora that keep the outbreak alive.

### 5. Containment becomes recovery

With the source removed and ecological support reduced, an outbreak can begin collapsing. Contamination recedes from its edges as the remaining material dies back.

Missed pockets still matter. Contaminated water, surviving flora, carcasses, and dormant ice can make an area more difficult to clear completely.

## Weather, water, and dormant contamination

An outbreak responds to its environment.

Rain changes airborne and water-based pathogen activity. Cold suppresses spread, flora growth, and spore activity, giving you an opportunity to contain a dangerous area.

**Contaminated ice is dormant:** it does not spread or expose you while frozen, but it still holds pathogen material. When it melts, it becomes contaminated water again.

Thawing can bring a surge of renewed activity. A frozen pond may look quiet while preserving the outbreak beneath its surface.

**Cold buys time. Complete cleanup still requires removing the contamination.**

## Investigating and containing an outbreak

The **Pathogen Sampler** helps you understand what is happening in the field.

Use it to investigate contaminated material, check source conditions, and distinguish between a source-fed outbreak, a self-sustaining ecosystem, and a collapsing zone. Climate readings also help identify dormant or thawing contamination.

Sampling hazardous material carries its own risks. Spore filters provide protection, but their durability needs attention.

To contain an outbreak:

1. **Find and stabilize the source** before it can keep feeding the contamination.
2. **Limit exposure** while investigating and clearing the area.
3. **Destroy pathogen flora** that supports the mature ecosystem.
4. **Sterilize contaminated terrain with fire** and clear contaminated remains.
5. **Check the area again** for surviving contamination, including water and frozen material.

The Sampler helps you tell whether your cleanup is actually driving the outbreak toward collapse.

## Treatment and survival

Treatment becomes more demanding as infection progresses.

* **Honey Bottle** — Can purge infection during the earliest **EXPOSED** stage and reduce pathogen exposure.
* **Golden Apple** — Requires **Weakness** and reverses infection progression during **EXPOSED** or **INCUBATING**. During **SYMPTOMATIC**, it also requires cauterization: **being on fire while protected by Fire Resistance**.
* **Enchanted Golden Apple** — Requires **Weakness**, provides stronger treatment, and can treat **SYMPTOMATIC** infection without cauterization.
* **TERMINAL** — Too late for treatment; the infection proceeds to bursting.

Active treatment reverses infection progression and can cause **Nausea** and **Hunger**. High or extreme exposure speeds up an existing infection, so leaving the contaminated area remains important even after treatment begins.

Exposure also decays after time away from hazards, and washing helps reduce it. Lowering exposure does not automatically cure an established infection.

## Surviving the outbreak

Investigate unusual growth before it becomes widespread. Treat leaking sources as urgent. Avoid contaminated water and respond to infection before symptoms become severe.

An empty source does not guarantee a safe area. A mature outbreak may already have enough flora to survive on its own, and frozen contamination can reactivate when it thaws.

**Find the source. Break the cycle. Reclaim the land.**
